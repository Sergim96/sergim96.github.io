System identificationT-RO · Conditionally accepted

Learning the robot’s physics, under real constraints.

I developed a Bayesian framework to jointly estimate robot states, inertial parameters and friction while respecting contacts and closed kinematic loops. A structured solver keeps the backward pass linear in the trajectory horizon.

Experiments on a B1 with a Z1 arm connect the identified models to better end-effector tracking and contact-rich motion.

≈50%

lower friction-parameter error with energy observations in a biped and quadruped simulation ablation.100 random initialisations per model; compared with the same estimator without energy observations.

State & parameter estimationRA-L · 2025

Estimating the payload.
Localising the robot.

Multiple contacts make localisation and payload identification tightly coupled. I developed a multiple-shooting estimator with physically consistent inertial parameters and analytical contact-dynamics derivatives.

The framework was evaluated against least-squares estimation, tested on demanding simulated motions and validated in experiments on a Go1 quadruped.

02 / Ongoing research

Research in progress.

Exploring contact dynamics, adaptive control and robot design. These clips show preliminary results in simulation.

Adaptive MPC Work in progress

Adapting as the robot’s world changes.

A changing load or slippery ground can make a familiar movement much harder. This research explores how adaptive control can help a robot maintain its balance and follow its intended motion as those conditions change.

The clips compare a fixed controller on the left with an adaptive controller on the right. The first shows a heavier, off-centre payload; the second shows reduced ground friction.

Differentiable simulation Work in progress

Exploring dexterous contact.

Dexterous manipulation depends on many small contacts between a hand and an object. This research explores contact dynamics for planning and understanding complex robot motion.

Here, a simulated Allegro hand turns a cube through 180° while keeping it close to its starting position. The slowed playback makes the coordinated finger and object motion easier to see.

Locomotion & reaching Work in progress

Finding steps to reach a target.

Talos is given a gripper-position target, with no predefined footsteps or walking sequence. The resulting motion combines stepping and changes in posture to bring its right hand towards the goal.

This research explores how robots can discover coordinated locomotion and reaching behaviours from a task goal. The gold marker shows the gripper target.

Exploratory simulation; hardware limits are not enforced.

Robot co-design Work in progress

Designing the robot around its motion.

A robot’s shape and actuation influence the movements it can perform. This research explores robot design and motion together, with the aim of creating robots better suited to their tasks.

The clip shows a walking trajectory for an optimised Kangaroo model, with its leg mechanisms visible as it takes each step.

05 / A little background

Curious about the maths.
Hands-on with the robot.

I’m a PhD researcher in the Robot Motor Intelligence Lab at Heriot-Watt University, supervised by Carlos Mastalli. My work sits at the intersection of physical modelling, numerical optimisation and robot experiments.

Before my PhD, I developed C++/ROS software and integrated sensors and hardware for cloth manipulation at IRI (CSIC–UPC). I also designed and tested a dynamically balanced compliant mechanism during a research internship at TU Delft.

I hold an MSc in Automatic Control and Robotics from UPC and degrees in Mechatronics Engineering and Industrial Electronics Engineering and Automation from UVic-UCC.
