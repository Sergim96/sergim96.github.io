01 / Selected research

Making models work
in the physical world.

My research brings dynamics, estimation and control together to identify what a robot is doing—and the physical parameters that explain its motion.

▶ Watch experiments

System identificationT-RO · Conditionally accepted

Learning the robot’s physics, under real constraints.

I developed a Bayesian framework to jointly estimate robot states, inertial parameters and friction while respecting contacts and closed kinematic loops. A structured solver keeps the backward pass linear in the trajectory horizon.

Experiments on a B1 with a Z1 arm connect the identified models to better end-effector tracking and contact-rich motion.

≈50%

lower friction-parameter error with energy observations in a biped and quadruped simulation ablation.100 random initialisations per model; compared with the same estimator without energy observations.

Multi-contact estimation · Humanoid simulation

State & parameter estimationRA-L · 2025

Estimating the payload.
Localising the robot.

Multiple contacts make localisation and payload identification tightly coupled. I developed a multiple-shooting estimator with physically consistent inertial parameters and analytical contact-dynamics derivatives.

The framework was evaluated against least-squares estimation, tested on demanding simulated motions and validated in experiments on a Go1 quadruped.

04 / A little background

Curious about the maths.
Hands-on with the robot.

I’m a PhD researcher in the Robot Motor Intelligence Lab at Heriot-Watt University, supervised by Carlos Mastalli. My work sits at the intersection of physical modelling, numerical optimisation and robot experiments.

Before my PhD, I developed C++/ROS software and integrated sensors and hardware for cloth manipulation at IRI (CSIC–UPC). I also designed and tested a dynamically balanced compliant mechanism during a research internship at TU Delft.

I hold an MSc in Automatic Control and Robotics from UPC and degrees in Mechatronics Engineering and Industrial Electronics Engineering and Automation from UVic-UCC.
